"Tell me about yourself" sounds simple. It isn't. It's an open-ended invitation for you to either nail the interview's first impression or fumble it immediately.

Most candidates treat it as a request for their life story. That's wrong. The interviewer wants to know: *are you the right person for this specific job?*

## The Formula That Works

The best answers follow a three-part structure called **Present → Past → Future**:

1. **Present**: Your current role or most recent relevant experience
2. **Past**: Key background that led you here
3. **Future**: Why you're excited about this specific opportunity

This keeps your answer focused, demonstrates trajectory, and ends on a note that's specifically relevant to the interviewer.

## A Real Example

**Weak answer:**
"I grew up in Ohio, went to Ohio State, majored in marketing, did a few internships, then got my first job at a startup..."

**Strong answer:**
"I'm currently a marketing manager at TechCo, where I lead our content and SEO strategy — we grew organic traffic 180% over 18 months. Before that, I cut my teeth at a small agency where I learned to run campaigns across multiple clients simultaneously. I'm looking to move into a director-level role now, and what draws me to this company specifically is your focus on product-led growth, which is an area I've been building expertise in."

Notice what the strong answer does:
- Leads with current impact, not job title
- Shows progression, not just history
- Ends with why *this job*, not just any job

## Tailor It Every Time

Your "tell me about yourself" answer should change for every interview. Pull 1-2 elements from the job description and work them in. Interviewers talk to dozens of candidates — the ones who clearly prepared for *this* role stand out immediately.

## Keep It to 90 Seconds

Practice your answer out loud. If it takes more than 90 seconds, cut it. Longer isn't more impressive — it's harder to listen to. A crisp, confident 60-90 second answer signals you know your own story and respect the interviewer's time.

## What Not to Say

- Don't recap your entire resume chronologically
- Don't talk about personal life unless you're making it relevant
- Don't say "I'm a hard worker" — show it with a specific result
- Don't end with "...and that's about it" — end with why you're excited about this role
