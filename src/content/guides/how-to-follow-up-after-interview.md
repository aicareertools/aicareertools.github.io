Following up after an interview is one of the most underused tools in a job seeker's arsenal. Done right, it reinforces your interest, differentiates you from other candidates, and keeps you top of mind.

## The Thank You Email: Send It Within 24 Hours

This isn't optional. Send a thank you email to every person who interviewed you within 24 hours of your interview. If you interviewed with a panel, email each person individually — not a group email.

**Template:**

> Subject: Thank you — [Your Name] / [Role Name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today about the [Role] position. I enjoyed learning more about [specific thing discussed — the team structure, a project they mentioned, etc.].
>
> Our conversation reinforced my enthusiasm for the role. In particular, [one specific detail from the interview that excited you] sounds like exactly the kind of challenge I'd thrive in.
>
> I look forward to hearing about next steps. Please let me know if you need anything else from me.
>
> Best,
> [Your Name]

Keep it short. Don't write three paragraphs. The goal is to leave a positive final impression, not to convince them with more content.

## The Status Check: If You Haven't Heard Back

If they said you'd hear back "by Friday" and it's now Monday, it's fine to follow up once.

**Template:**

> Subject: Following up — [Your Name] / [Role Name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I wanted to follow up on the [Role] position. I understand hiring timelines can shift, and I remain very interested in the opportunity.
>
> Could you give me a quick update on where things stand? Happy to provide any additional information that would be helpful.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your Name]

This is professional, not pushy. You're giving them an easy out ("timelines shift") while reminding them you exist.

## How Long to Wait Before Following Up

| They said... | Follow up after... |
|---|---|
| "You'll hear from us by [date]" | 3 business days after that date |
| "1-2 weeks" | 10 business days |
| Nothing specific | 1 week after the interview |

## After a Rejection

Yes, follow up after a rejection too. Thank them for their time and ask for feedback. Most won't give it, but occasionally you'll get genuinely useful information. More importantly, it keeps the door open — hiring needs change.

> Thank you for letting me know. I appreciated the opportunity to interview, and I enjoyed learning about [Company]. If you'd be open to it, any feedback on my candidacy would be valuable for my job search.

## What Not to Do

- Don't follow up more than twice — it signals desperation
- Don't follow up via LinkedIn AND email — pick one
- Don't mention you have other offers unless it's true and you're prepared to follow through
- Don't express disappointment or frustration in writing, ever

The follow-up email is a small thing that takes 5 minutes and has a disproportionate impact on your candidacy. Don't skip it.
