A strong résumé is more than a list of past jobs—it’s a targeted sales pitch that shows hiring managers you have the exact skills they’re looking for. The way you present those skills can mean the difference between a quick rejection and a callback. Below is a step‑by‑step guide that turns vague skill claims into clear, quantifiable evidence of your value.

## Understand the Role and Audience

Before you even write a word, reverse‑engineer the job posting. Use the [Job Description Analyzer](/tools/job-description-analyzer) to pull out the most frequently mentioned hard and soft skills. Create a quick spreadsheet: column A lists the skill, column B notes how many times it appears, column C flags whether it’s a hard or soft skill. Prioritize skills that appear at least twice or are marked “must-have.”  

**Example:**  
- **Job posting for a Marketing Manager** lists “SEO,” “content strategy,” “data analysis,” and “team leadership” repeatedly.  
- Your spreadsheet will show SEO (hard, 3 times), content strategy (hard, 2 times), data analysis (hard, 2 times), team leadership (soft, 2 times).  

Focus on these four skills. If you’re a junior candidate, you can also list related skills like “keyword research” or “Google Analytics” under SEO.  

### Actionable Tip  
When you’re unsure about a skill’s relevance, test it on the résumé by asking a mentor or a friend: “Does this skill make sense for this role?” If the answer is no, remove it.

## Categorize Your Skills

Divide skills into three buckets: **Core Technical, Transferable, and Personal**. This structure lets recruiters scan quickly and see where you excel.

| Bucket | What to Include | Why |
|--------|-----------------|-----|
| Core Technical | Software, certifications, industry tools | Shows you meet the minimum requirement |
| Transferable | Project management, communication, problem solving | Demonstrates adaptability across roles |
| Personal | Creativity, resilience, curiosity | Humanizes you and shows cultural fit |

**Example:**  
For a software engineer applying to a fintech startup:  
- Core Technical: Java, Spring Boot, SQL, AWS, Docker  
- Transferable: Agile Scrum, cross‑functional collaboration, code reviews  
- Personal: Passion for financial literacy, mentor for coding bootcamps  

Place the Core Technical section first. If the job emphasizes “cloud experience,” list “AWS Certified Solutions Architect” prominently.  

### Actionable Tip  
Use bullet points for each skill and keep them concise—one line per skill. Avoid long paragraphs that bury the skill in text.

## Prioritize and Quantify

Listing a skill alone isn’t enough; you must demonstrate mastery. Pair each skill with a brief achievement that quantifies impact. Use the **STAR** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) method, but keep it to one line.

**Format:**  
`Skill – Result (metric)`

**Example:**  
- **SEO – Increased organic traffic by 45% in 6 months**  
- **Java – Refactored legacy code, cutting build time from 15 min to 3 min**  
- **Team Leadership – Managed a 5‑person team, delivering 4 projects ahead of schedule**  

If you’re new to a skill, show rapid progress:  
- **Data Analysis – Completed 3 Tableau dashboards for quarterly sales, reducing reporting time by 30%**  

### Actionable Tip  
When you can’t provide a metric, use qualitative impact: “improved user engagement” or “enhanced cross‑team collaboration.” Still, aim for numbers whenever possible.

## Format and Placement

The visual hierarchy of your résumé should guide the recruiter’s eye. Use a clear, consistent layout:

1. **Header** – Name, contact info, LinkedIn, portfolio.  
2. **Summary** – 2–3 sentences that spotlight your most relevant skills.  
3. **Core Skills** – Bulleted list or two columns, grouped by category.  
4. **Professional Experience** – Job title, company, dates, followed by 3–5 bullet points that tie back to your listed skills.  
5. **Education & Certifications** – Include relevant credentials.  

**Design Tips:**  
- Use bold for skill names, keep the rest regular font.  
- Avoid dense blocks; leave 0.5–1 cm margins.  
- Choose a clean font (Calibri, Garamond) and a maximum of two font sizes.  

**Example Layout:**

```
John Doe
Software Engineer | john.doe@email.com | +1‑555‑123‑4567 | LinkedIn | Portfolio

SUMMARY
Dynamic software engineer with 3+ years in fintech, specializing in scalable cloud solutions and data‑driven product development.

CORE SKILLS
- Core Technical: Java, Spring Boot, SQL, AWS, Docker
- Transferable: Agile Scrum, Code Review, Cross‑Functional Collaboration
- Personal: Analytical Thinking, Mentorship, Continuous Learning

PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE
Fintech Solutions – Software Engineer | Jan 2021 – Present
- Java – Refactored legacy code, cutting build time from 15 min to 3 min
- AWS – Deployed microservices, improving uptime by 99.9%
- Agile Scrum – Led sprint planning, boosting velocity by 20%

...
```

Run your résumé through the [Resume Analyzer](/tools/resume-analyzer) to get a quick readability score and keyword match rate. Adjust accordingly.
