The resume summary is the most valuable and most wasted real estate on your resume. Positioned right at the top, it's the first thing a hiring manager reads — and most candidates squander it with platitudes like "results-driven professional with excellent communication skills."

Here's how to make yours work.

## Should You Include a Summary?

Only if you can make it specific. If your summary is generic, it actively hurts you by making the reader feel like you didn't personalize your application. Leave it out and go straight to your experience section.

Include a summary if:
- You're changing careers and need to reframe your background
- You're a senior candidate whose top skills aren't obvious from the job titles alone
- The role has specific requirements you want to highlight upfront

## The Formula for a Strong Summary

Three sentences, 3-5 lines maximum:

**Sentence 1**: Who you are + your most impressive credential or achievement
**Sentence 2**: Your core skills or areas of expertise most relevant to this role
**Sentence 3**: What you're looking to do next (connects you to this specific role)

## Examples by Level

**Entry Level:**
> Recent Computer Science graduate with a published open-source library (2.4k GitHub stars) and two SWE internships at Series B startups. Proficient in Python, React, and PostgreSQL. Eager to apply production-grade software skills to [Company]'s backend infrastructure team.

**Mid-Level:**
> Marketing manager with 6 years driving B2B SaaS growth. Built and scaled a content engine that contributed $4M in pipeline last year across SEO, paid, and email channels. Looking to bring that cross-channel experience to a senior role at a company with strong product-market fit.

**Career Changer:**
> Former high school teacher transitioning to UX design, backed by a Google UX Design Certificate and 3 portfolio projects. Bringing 8 years of curriculum design and user empathy directly into product work. Seeking a junior UX role where I can grow fast.

**Senior/Executive:**
> VP of Engineering with 15 years scaling engineering organizations from 5 to 200+. Led platform migrations, built distributed teams across 6 countries, and delivered $40M ARR products. Now seeking a CTO role at a Series B or C company ready to scale.

## What to Cut

- "Results-driven" — everyone claims this
- "Excellent communication skills" — prove it with your writing
- "Team player" — meaningless
- "Passionate about [vague thing]" — show it, don't say it
- "Looking for a challenging opportunity" — your job search is not an interesting detail to them

## How to Tailor It

Your summary should change slightly for every application. At minimum, update the target role and any company-specific language. Spend 10 minutes on this — it's the section with the highest ROI per minute of effort.
