Product manager interviews are notoriously broad. You might be asked about user research, technical architecture, metrics, strategy, and behavioral scenarios — all in the same 45-minute conversation. Here's what to actually prepare for.

## The Four Categories of PM Interview Questions

**1. Product Sense** — "Design an app for X" or "Improve our [feature]"
**2. Analytical** — "How would you measure success of Y feature?"
**3. Execution** — "You're halfway through a sprint and requirements change. What do you do?"
**4. Behavioral** — "Tell me about a time you disagreed with an engineer."

Most candidates over-prepare on product sense and under-prepare on execution and behavioral. Interviewers notice.

## Top 20 Questions + How to Answer Them

### Product Sense

**1. Design a product for [unusual user group]**
Framework: Start with users → identify their top pain points → define success metrics → propose 3 solutions → recommend one with tradeoffs.

**2. How would you improve [Company's product]?**
Always lead with data: "I'd first look at where users are dropping off." Propose improvements based on user pain, not features you personally want.

**3. What's your favorite product and why?**
Pick something you genuinely use. Structure: what problem it solves, why the UX is excellent, what you'd change.

### Analytical

**4. How do you define success for this feature?**
Lead with: "What's the user goal? What's the business goal?" Then define 2-3 metrics. Include a guardrail metric (what you're trying NOT to impact).

**5. Our [metric] dropped 20% last week. Walk me through how you'd investigate.**
Framework: rule out instrumentation errors → segment by platform/geo/user type → look at recent launches → check external factors.

**6. How do you prioritize your roadmap?**
Show a framework (RICE, ICE, OKR-alignment) but explain that you adapt it based on company stage and stakeholder input.

### Execution

**7. How do you handle scope creep?**
Good answer: acknowledge the source of the request, evaluate impact on timeline, make a trade-off explicit to stakeholders, and document the decision.

**8. An engineer tells you a key feature will take 3x longer than estimated. What do you do?**
Understand why first. Then: can we scope it down? Can we ship a MVP? Communicate timeline impact to stakeholders immediately.

### Behavioral

**9. Tell me about a time you launched something that failed.**
Be specific, honest about your role, and focus on what you learned. Interviewers are testing self-awareness, not perfection.

**10. Describe a time you disagreed with a stakeholder.**
Structure: the disagreement → how you presented your case with data → how it was resolved → what you'd do differently.

## Day-Of Tips

- Bring 5-7 "stories" from your past work that can be adapted to multiple question types
- Ask clarifying questions before answering design/strategy questions — it shows PM instincts
- Think out loud; interviewers are evaluating your reasoning, not just your answer
- Leave 5 minutes for your questions; asking nothing signals low interest
