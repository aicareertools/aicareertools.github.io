Software engineering is one of the most competitive job markets. A strong resume doesn't just list your experience — it proves impact. Here's what separates the resumes that get callbacks from the ones that don't.

## Lead With Impact, Not Responsibilities

Every bullet on your resume should answer: *so what?*

**Weak:** "Developed new API endpoints for the user service."

**Strong:** "Built 12 REST API endpoints for the user service, reducing average response time by 40% and handling 50k+ daily requests without downtime."

Numbers don't have to be exact — reasonable estimates are fine. What matters is that you're demonstrating scale and outcome, not just activity.

## The Right Format for SWE Resumes

- **Length**: One page for under 10 years of experience; two pages maximum for senior roles
- **Order**: Summary (optional) → Skills → Experience → Education → Projects
- **Skills section**: List languages, frameworks, tools. Group them logically. Don't list things you'd be embarrassed to be asked about.
- **Font**: Readable 10-12pt. No columns in ATS-destined resumes — they confuse parsers.

## ATS Optimization

Most large companies use Applicant Tracking Systems that scan resumes before human eyes see them. To pass ATS:

- Use keywords from the job description verbatim
- Avoid tables, headers/footers, images, and unusual formatting
- Use standard section names: "Experience," "Education," "Skills"
- Save as PDF (unless the application specifies otherwise)

## What to Do With GitHub / Portfolio

Link to your GitHub profile at the top of your resume. But don't assume they'll click through — your best projects should have 1-2 bullet points in a **Projects** section:

- **Project Name** (github.com/you/project) | React, Node.js, PostgreSQL
  - Built X that does Y, resulting in Z

Pinned repos should have good READMEs with screenshots. First impressions of your code matter.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Listing tools you barely know**: If it's on your resume, expect to be asked about it
- **Generic summary statements**: "Passionate developer with 5 years of experience" is noise. Skip the summary or make it highly specific.
- **Job duty bullets**: "Responsible for maintaining the database" tells them nothing. What did you actually do?
- **Including GPA after 5 years**: Unless it's exceptional and you're early-career, leave it off
- **No dates on experience**: Gaps are obvious if you omit dates — include them

## The "Reverse Chronology" Rule

Most recent and most relevant experience goes first. If you have a side project that's more relevant than your last job, put it in a **Projects** section above **Experience**.

## How to Handle Career Gaps

Short gaps (under 6 months) don't need explanation on a resume. Longer gaps: address briefly in your cover letter, or note freelance/contract/learning work during that period. Honesty is always the right call — interviewers will ask.

A strong software engineer resume is less about the format and more about proving, in specific measurable terms, that you made things better wherever you worked.
