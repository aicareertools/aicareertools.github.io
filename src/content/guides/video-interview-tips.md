Video interviews on Zoom and Microsoft Teams have become the norm, but they still carry unique challenges that can trip even seasoned candidates. Unlike a face‑to‑face meeting, the digital format magnifies every technical glitch, lighting flaw, and awkward pause. If you want to turn a virtual screen into a stage where you shine, you need a clear plan, the right tools, and practiced habits. Below is a step‑by‑step guide that turns the anxiety of video interviews into a confident, polished performance—ready for any hiring manager, anywhere in the world.

## ## 1. Prep Your Tech and Environment

**Test everything the night before.**  
- **Video & audio**: Open Zoom or Teams, join a test call, and record a short clip. Check that the camera is at eye level and the microphone picks up your voice without distortion.  
- **Internet speed**: Run a speed test (e.g., Speedtest.net). Aim for at least 3 Mbps upload/download. If Wi‑Fi is spotty, plug in an Ethernet cable or use a mobile hotspot as backup.  
- **Backup plan**: Have a second device ready (tablet or smartphone) and a backup headset. If the primary fails, you can switch quickly without losing the interview’s flow.

**Create a distraction‑free zone.**  
- **Noise**: Use a quiet room, close doors, and turn off notifications on your phone and computer. If background noise is unavoidable, consider a noise‑cancelling microphone.  
- **Lighting**: Position a soft light source (e.g., desk lamp) facing you, not behind. Natural daylight from a window works best, but avoid direct sunlight that can cast harsh shadows.  
- **Camera angle**: The lens should be at or slightly above eye level. If you’re using a phone, prop it on a stack of books or a stand to keep it steady.

*Example:* Jane, a product manager, set up her interview space by placing a lamp on her desk, using a headset, and running a quick test call the night before. When the call came, her video was crisp, her voice clear, and she avoided the dreaded “my Wi‑Fi died” moment.

## ## 2. Master Camera Presence and Body Language

**Dress like you’re in a physical interview.**  
- Even if the interview is remote, wear professional attire. A crisp shirt or blouse conveys seriousness and respects the interviewer's time.  
- Avoid bright patterns that can cause a moiré effect on camera.

**Use intentional eye contact.**  
- Look at the camera when speaking, not the screen. This simulates direct eye contact and helps build rapport.  
- Practice by recording yourself and reviewing where you look. Adjust your headset or camera height if needed.

**Keep your body language open.**  
- Sit up straight, shoulders relaxed, and keep your hands visible but not fidgeting.  
- Nod to show you’re listening; pause briefly before answering to collect your thoughts.  
- Avoid crossing arms or looking away; these cues can signal disinterest.

*Example:* Alex, a software engineer, practiced speaking into a webcam while looking at the lens. He recorded a mock interview, noticed he was looking at the screen, and corrected it. The result was a natural, engaging presence that impressed his hiring manager.

**Analyze the posting before you answer.**  
- Use the [Job Description Analyzer](/tools/job-description-analyzer) to extract key skills, keywords, and company values.  
- Map your own experiences to those keywords.

## ## 4. Manage the Technical Flow During the Call

**Start with a friendly greeting.**  
- As soon as the call begins, say, “Hi, I’m [Name]. Thank you for the opportunity.” This establishes rapport and signals confidence.  
- If you’re using Teams, you can share your screen for your résumé or portfolio right away, but only after the interviewer says it’s okay.

**Keep the screen organized.**  
- Close unnecessary tabs and applications to avoid accidental sharing.  
- Disable “pop‑up” notifications in both Zoom and Teams.  
- If you need to reference a document, keep it in a single folder and share it only when asked.

**Handle hiccups gracefully.**  
- If the connection drops, apologize, describe what you’re doing to reconnect, and stay calm.  
- If the interviewer asks a question you can’t answer immediately, say, “That’s an excellent question. I’ll need to think about it for a moment.” Then take a brief pause, not a long one.
